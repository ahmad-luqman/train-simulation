# Phase 3 — Movement and dispatch

**Status correction, 10 September 2026:** This is an initial implementation, not completed anti-collision dispatch. The [collision and routing audit](PHASE_3_COLLISION_AUDIT.md) reproduces overlapping initial trains, a physical main/loop chassis collision despite unique reservations, acceptance of the collision save, and permanent default-fleet congestion. The previous all-fleet progress test was weakened to isolated services. Complete [corrective Phase 3A](ADVANCED_GAME_ROADMAP.md#phase-3a--collision-safety-and-automatic-routing-corrective-milestone), including physical parallel tracks and junction routing, before Phase 4.

## Implementation plan

1. Add a renderer-independent dispatch module for physical train dimensions, traction/braking, actual route history, resource ownership and circular-wait diagnostics. Keep fixed 50 ms simulation ticks.
2. Give each physical track an independent block. Protect shared endpoint turnouts and individual platforms, retaining protection until the rear clears. Acquire resources transactionally and apply direction rules only at safe boundaries.
3. Integrate acceleration, mass/load and gradient resistance, curvature speed limits and braking envelopes. Use an explicit distance scale for displayed km/h. Preserve actual consist paths across service edits and use reversible operation at terminals.
4. Add periodic departure slots, minimum headway, platform preferences, passenger/freight priorities and bounded aging. Provide safe targeted dispatch and parallel-track recovery without deleting occupied reservations.
5. Expose a dispatcher panel with occupancy, wait reasons, queues, punctuality, throughput and controls. Extend the existing green/brass interface and update world signals and wagon positions.
6. Persist motion, route history, schedules and reservations in validated version 3 saves. Migrate versions 1/2 atomically and retain their browser storage keys.
7. Verify long consists, opposing loop movements, restrictive signals at 8×, terminal reversal, holds, scheduling, circular waits and save/load continuity. Run the complete regression suite, typecheck, lint and production build before committing.

## Verification and boundaries

This is a miniature railway dispatcher, with one block per physical edge and conservative shared endpoint turnouts; arbitrary mid-edge signals and detailed cab simulation remain outside this phase.

## Implemented behavior at `b1c2d05` (not a physical safety guarantee)

- Each edge is one exclusive bidirectional block. A passing loop has its own block; the old `block` field is retained only as its parent construction reference. Endpoint turnouts are shared exclusive resources. A destination platform is reserved before departure, and resource expiry uses an estimated consist distance. The audit shows these logical reservations do not cover all physically conflicting movements. Direction changes require an empty track; route planning observes one-way rules.
- Resource claims are checked before departure. Passenger priority gets a small advantage, waiting services gain up to 100 priority points, and **Dispatch next** wins the next departure permitted by the current logical checks. Priority cannot bypass held trains, direction rules, headway, departure slots or occupied resources. A platform preference change does not move an existing reservation.
- Speed integrates at 50 ms, including at 8×. Traction depends on mass and load; grade opposes uphill acceleration; curvature and consist size restrict speed. Braking envelopes include a tick of reaction distance and clamp movement at the stop line. One scene unit represents 50/9 physical metres for motion; 2.5 units/s is 50 km/h. Existing construction prices and geometry quantities retain their original scene-unit contract.
- A train records the path it actually traversed. Wagons sample this history, which is pruned only after rear clearance. Reassignment at a station retains the incoming path and timetable. At a reversal, the old rear becomes the leading end and the train operates backward; the implementation intends to preserve vehicle positions; current reversal pose tests sample only part of the rendered consist. The locomotive does not jump to the other end. Track must be longer than the consist plus turnout clearance. Wagon additions require a forward-facing station stop with a clear approach and extend existing rear-clearance protection.
- Services support loading dwell, first departure in elapsed simulation seconds, repeating departure slots (0 means depart when ready), minimum headway on the same track and direction, priority, and station platform preferences. Punctuality measures departure within 2 seconds of its due time. Throughput is completed scheduled calls per elapsed simulation minute, not cargo profit.
- The dispatcher includes a live block map, textual ownership ledger, queues with named blockers, punctuality/throughput, timetable forms, direction controls and targeted overrides. Train details display actual measured motion speed and waiting reasons. Scene signals read physical block IDs; wheels continue moving during signal braking.
- Circular waits are detected from actual blocking ownership. Recovery actions are explicit: release a held train, select an available parallel track before departure, edit a held station service, buy platform capacity, or turn a stationary train back. Turn-back requires a bidirectional station-to-station track and complete turnout clearance. It preserves poses and occupied blocks, replaces the itinerary with a recovery shuttle and requests a hold at the previous station. A recovery movement still brakes for an unavailable destination platform or junction.
- Version 3 saves include velocities, route history, direction of operation, total travel, schedules, priority overrides, wait state, throughput counters, headway clocks and resource release distances. Validation checks ownership uniqueness, required blocks/turnouts/platforms, rear expiry distances, path continuity and existing network/accounting invariants on a detached candidate. Versions 1/2 retain positions, balances and network records, start at zero velocity and rebuild safe current-approach protection. Older formats cannot reconstruct an actual historical path; incompatible overlapping turnout positions are rejected atomically. The original browser slots remain untouched.

## Design choices and limits

The Phase 2 starter timetable can congest under full-consist protection. The original simulation released protection as soon as the locomotive reached a station, making those conflicts invisible. The dense-fleet regression now checks explicit circular waits and physical recovery; every locomotive is also tested for sustained deliveries on its unobstructed service. The passing-loop scenario demonstrates repeated opposing operation with enough platform capacity. A loop alone cannot cure a separate platform bottleneck.

Stations use logical platform resources; this phase does not add individually modelled platform approach tracks. All endpoint movements share one conservative turnout lock, and trains stop at each edge boundary, including transit nodes. Initial trains are described as staged at their starting nodes, but remain visibly colocated and initially unreserved; this is an audited defect. Holds remain emergency stops, while **Stop at next station** uses the normal braking approach. Automatic dispatch currently allocates movements using resource IDs; it does not establish physical safety or automatically choose a feasible alternative route. The default timetable stalls, so the concurrent-fleet acceptance is reopened. Freeform blocks, closer same-track headways, locomotive run-arounds and detailed cab simulation remain deferred.

## Historical verification results and audit limits

- 39 regression tests pass, but the audit reproductions expose defects outside their assertions. These tests do not close the physical collision or concurrent-fleet gates. Coverage includes 16 new dispatch scenarios: traction/grade/curvature and speed scale; six-car rear clearance; repeated opposing passing-loop operation; restrictive junction braking at 8×; terminal reversal without vehicle jumps; retained wagon paths and timetables after reassignment; named platform queues and capacity relief; departure slots and headway; priority/aging/override arbitration; direction safety and planning; circular-wait recovery; parallel-route override; corrupt motion/reservation rejection; version 2 migration; and repeated save/load throughout a long train’s reversing journey.
- TypeScript, lint, production build and local HTTP route smoke check pass. Production retains the existing large-chunk warning and Vinext route-classification notice.
- Browser interaction, WebGL visual acceptance, touch/keyboard usability and measured GPU performance remain unverified. The active Sites environment permits browser testing only when explicitly requested; no browser-testing request was made in this pass. This does not close the outstanding Phase 0/1 visual/performance baseline.
