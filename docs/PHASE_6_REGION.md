# Phase 6 — progression and a living region

## Implementation plan

1. Add a renderer-independent region state and deterministic calendar. Keep sandbox as the compatible default, with independent switches for progression, growth, weather and events. Campaign starts with two affordable engines on the existing regional infrastructure; unowned service slots remain available for later purchases.
2. Teach selection, construction, service assignment, deliveries, congestion recovery and reinvestment through successful simulation commands and observed results. Present the next action alongside a recovery path. Unlock infrastructure, freight and express research through deliveries, development and reliable departures, with explicit prices and dependencies.
3. Grow towns only after successive supplied windows. Cap levels, population, demand and additional buildings. Increase actual consumption and passenger generation without changing cargo conservation or storage bounds.
4. Derive seasons, fog/rain/snow and river variation from seed and simulation time. Apply small speed reductions without weakening braking. Forecast bridge inspections and demand festivals; block only new route admissions, allowing already-authorized trains to clear. Provide alternate routes, safe waiting and paid inspection deferral.
5. Ship mountain freight, junction congestion, river expansion and passenger punctuality scenarios with concrete objectives, deadlines, frozen results, achievements and a continue-after-setback flow.
6. Persist version 8 region state, migrate versions 5–7 to compatible sandbox settings, and reject malformed state atomically. Test campaign commands, progression, growth/conservation, deterministic weather/events, scenario success/failure, restoration and safety. Run the full regression suite, typecheck, lint and production build; record evidence and remaining acceptance limits.

## Design improvements

Use the existing ledger as delivery evidence, real dispatch departures for reliability and real commands for tutorial actions. Keep weather derived rather than serializing cosmetic random particles. Do not rebuild track topology for town growth. Keep scenarios on the validated physical network rather than introducing untested miniature layouts. Preserve sandbox behavior and old save slots.

## Delivered behavior

- **Campaign:** $145,000, two owned and initially held engines, a Millbrook grain service and a Grand Junction–Riverside box-wagon service. The remaining slots are vacant. The original regional network is retained as inherited infrastructure, preserving validated geometry and offering expansion choices. Both starters form a real grain → flour → consumer chain. The normal first launch remains the existing full-fleet sandbox; start a campaign or challenge from **Region & goals → New campaign, sandbox or challenge**, or the existing New railway dialog.
- **Tutorial:** selection is recorded by a real roster/map selection; construction and service steps require successful commands; a delivery requires accepted cargo; congestion requires a traffic wait, a dispatch intervention and a subsequent departure; reinvestment requires an actual paid purchase after revenue begins. The suggested new line is Grand Junction–Riverside on the negative bend side, then set to **Both directions** in Dispatcher before applying it as a preferred service track. The suggested Millbrook parallel line was rejected by the existing clearance validator during implementation and was not shipped as tutorial guidance.
- **Research:** Civil engineering costs $6,000 after 42 units and unlocks workshop construction and controlled crossovers. Industrial steam costs $12,000 after Civil engineering, 168 units and a growing town; it unlocks heavy/mountain engines. Express era costs $16,000 after Civil engineering and 12 departures at 70% punctuality. Research availability is enforced in simulation commands and explained in the fleet office. Purchased engines retain the existing cargo, route, safety and maintenance rules. Research has no passive timer.
- **Growth:** 24 accepted units in each of two consecutive **600-second** windows adds one level, capped at three. A ten-minute window accommodates the measured roughly nine-minute starter freight round trip; the initial five-minute proposal did not. Population grows from 800 to at most 1,520, with up to four additional houses per level subject to track/map clearance. Growth increases passenger generation and consumer absorption while storage remains capped at 180. House groups preserve independent visibility through batching; growth does not rebuild track topology. Player-built station towns also support expansion.
- **Weather:** a persisted unsigned seed and simulation time determine 15-minute seasons and three-minute weather periods. Clear, fog, rain and winter snow have maximum speed factors of 1.0, 0.90, 0.92 and 0.85; the existing braking and swept-clearance rules remain unchanged. Fog affects scene and water consistently. River variation is bounded between −0.10 and +0.16 world units. Rain/snow use fixed buffers, with 80 particles on low and 240 otherwise. Pausing freezes weather motion; forecasts and conditions continue identically after restore.
- **Events:** each 20-minute cycle forecasts one existing river bridge inspection, normally at minute seven for 120 seconds. Existing authorized trains may clear; only new route admissions are rejected, and alternate paths remain available. Pay $2,500 before the inspection to defer it once by five minutes, wait safely, or reroute. A four-minute town festival is announced for minutes 13–17 and raises passenger generation and consumer demand. Neither event destroys infrastructure or cargo.
- **Scenarios and results:** Mountain freight requires 126 Granite Ridge coal delivered to Grand Junction in 60 minutes; Junction relief requires 210 units and observed recovery after a dispatch intervention in 40 minutes; Across the river requires a bridge-bearing purchase and 84 delivered units over player-built track in 50 minutes; On time express requires 252 passengers, 12 departures and 70% punctuality in 40 minutes. Results freeze accepted deliveries, operating profit, punctuality, departures and the three largest waiting categories. Failed challenges allow continued play and new attempts. Five achievements cover delivery, growth, reliability, campaign graduation and a scenario win.
- **Persistence:** version 8 saves region settings, seed, supply windows, tutorial evidence, research, interventions, achievements, event deferrals and results. Regional delivery counters reconcile against the existing ledger, which now records whether an accepted load traversed player-built track. Detached validation rejects malformed region state without touching the running game. Versions 5–7 migrate to the compatible sandbox with regional effects off and without relocating vehicles. Load checks v8 before preserved v7 and older slots. Sandbox independently controls progression, growth, weather and events; unlimited purchasing funds are restricted to sandbox. Campaigns/scenarios retain financing and forgiving overdrafts for recovery.

## Verification

- **105/105 automated tests pass** in 90.1 seconds on 11 September 2026, preserving the previous 91 tests and adding 14 Phase 6 tests. Existing version-output assertions now expect v8; the economy-only test clock explicitly ticks the region after advancing time. No original progress or collision assertion was removed.
- New tests cover the complete tutorial/campaign win through real commands; all four scenario wins; frozen missed results and continued play; research gates and atomic failures; growth, capped development and inventory conservation; seed/frame-rate/pause determinism; migration and corrupt-save rejection; announced/active/deferred inspections; and a full twelve-train 3,600-second region run. The latter asserts physical separation every tick, denies newly admitted inspected routes, restores at both checkpoints and requires every service to make additional calls in each 1,800-second window.
- **Typecheck, lint and production build pass.** The build retains the existing bundle-size warning. The local route responds HTTP 200.
- [Recorded campaign/scenario benchmark](benchmarks/phase6-region.json), reproducible with `npm run benchmark:region`: campaign completes at 3,723 simulated seconds, delivers 546 units, earns $30,628 operating profit and retains $101,924 after construction/research, with 86.2% punctuality. Mountain, junction, river and passenger challenges finish at 2,056, 1,464, 501 and 1,450 seconds respectively, before their deadlines. Headless cost ranges from approximately 0.030 to 0.063 ms/tick for these one/two-engine scenarios on an Apple M4 Max while regression tests run concurrently. This is not a GPU or full-fleet performance claim.

## Remaining acceptance

Browser interaction, screenshots, mobile layout and GPU profiling remain open. The Sites environment instructions require explicit browser-testing authorization; it was requested during implementation but not received. Automated simulation playthroughs and compilation do not certify visual acceptance. The next Phase 7 work should record a browser walkthrough of all tutorial controls, all quality/weather modes, grown towns at map/follow distances and scenario results, then establish the still-open hardware/frame-time baseline.

Campaign balancing is supported by a deterministic scripted playthrough, not a first-time-player usability study. The inherited map intentionally remains available; the campaign limits the owned fleet and gates advanced purchases instead of replacing proven physical geometry. Seasonal demand is a bounded throughput change, not a speculative price market. Town storage limits and all existing safety protections remain authoritative.
